# Performance Testing Standard

Use this skill to determine whether a named system/revision meets its approved performance objectives under a defined workload, and to report the limits of that evidence. Do not choose product SLOs, redesign the system, assume a load tool or claim production capacity from an unqualified environment.

## Modes and inputs

- **Plan:** derive experiments, case oracles, workload and evidence criteria from approved requirements/design. Test Analysis may use this mode without an executable environment or permission to generate load; record future execution prerequisites with owners. Missing material target or workload decisions block the dependent plan; never invent thresholds to finish it.
- **Implement-test:** create or revise authorized scripts/configuration/data preparation within the assigned write scope. Preparing a test does not authorize sending traffic, provisioning infrastructure or changing monitoring.
- **Execute:** verify the named target, revision, environment, actual workload/data, generator/instrumentation readiness, duration and load limits, abort/recovery/cleanup plan, evidence destination and explicit authorization for the target and generated load. Shared/production use and fault injection require their applicable explicit scope; ordinary coding authorization does not imply them.
- **Review:** assess retained run evidence against its original requirements, revisions, workload and environment. Identify missing evidence and comparison limits without implying a new run or changing acceptance thresholds.

Use approved source/case IDs and exact objectives; if a TA plan is supplied, preserve its allocation. During analysis, use draft case IDs until plan readiness is established. A formal TA/OpenSpec artifact or lifecycle status is not universally required. A comparative regression verdict additionally requires a trustworthy comparable baseline. Synthetic or authorized sanitized data must represent relevant cardinality, distribution, payload sizes, tenant mix and read/write behavior without exposing secrets or private production records.

## Mandatory workflow

**Complete steps 1 through 7 in order for every assignment.** Record mode, scope and each outcome, including focused work. Confirm reused evidence against current sources/revisions. Use `Not Applicable` only with a reason. Missing required inputs or permission are `Blocked`, with an owner/next action; stop dependent work and mark it `Pending`. Independent authorized planning/review may continue. Revisit affected steps after changes. In planning/build mode, execution steps produce explicit procedures and prerequisites, not fabricated results.

1. **Define the decision and acceptance criteria.** Identify source/case IDs, user/business operation, target revision, environment and tested boundary. Capture approved latency distributions/percentiles, throughput, error/timeout budget, resource/cost or recovery criteria and their measurement windows/populations as applicable. Define functional assertions so fast incorrect responses cannot pass. Record comparison purpose and baseline needs; do not invent SLOs, budgets or approval.
2. **Select the experiment and workload.** Use [experiment selection](references/experiment-and-evidence.md#experiment-selection) to choose only the test types needed by the question. Specify request/journey mix, payload/cardinality, data growth, concurrency or arrival model, think time, duration, ramp/steady/cooldown phases, warm-up, cold/warm cache state and repetitions. Distinguish open arrival-driven traffic from closed concurrency-driven traffic; define intended versus achieved work and how delayed, dropped or unfinished work is counted. No single short load run proves stress, soak and recovery properties.
3. **Design measurement and qualify prerequisites.** Identify target and generator topology/versions, dependency quotas, connection limits, time synchronization and timestamp boundaries, metric populations/tags, raw-result retention, tracing/sampling overhead and bottleneck signals. Establish generator headroom and workload feasibility, representative environment/data and cleanup, acceptance versus safety abort thresholds, monitoring/escalation ownership and post-abort recovery. For planned work record qualification tasks; before execution verify them and the explicit target/load/fault authorization. Choose tooling from the protocol and existing project conventions; verify version-specific commands when implementing.
4. **Prepare and run only the authorized scope.** In execution, verify functional preflight and instrumentation, then run the declared phases within rate/concurrency/duration/target limits. Retain script/config versions, commands, timestamps and raw metrics, and observe system and generator health. Stop at approved abort conditions or unexpected impact; record partial results and restoration actions. Do not silently raise limits or retry a dangerous run. In implement-test mode prepare assets only; in plan mode define this procedure; in review mode inspect recorded execution provenance.
5. **Assess validity before interpreting performance.** Reconcile offered, started, completed, failed, timed-out, dropped and unfinished work. Separate generator saturation, network/environment effects and target degradation; an achieved rate below the required workload cannot establish a passing capacity claim. Report distributions and tails per relevant operation/population, errors, queueing and resource saturation over time; do not conceal failed requests, average independent percentile values, or combine unrelated phases into a misleading pass. Confirm adequate sample size/repetitions and comparable measurement settings; identify uncertainty rather than asserting significance without evidence.
6. **Compare, diagnose and verify recovery.** Compare baseline and target only with controlled workload/data/configuration/environment and matching measurement windows, exposing unavoidable differences and noise. Tie bottleneck hypotheses to observed metrics/traces rather than correlations alone; route design/code/objective changes to owners. Verify required post-load health, backlog drain, functional integrity and authorized cleanup. Repeat only affected experiments justified by a correction or remaining uncertainty and within authorization; retain failed/aborted runs and do not extrapolate beyond measured conditions.
7. **Report the result and hand off.** Return source/case/revision traceability, experiment/workload, environment and generator qualification, acceptance/abort criteria, commands/configuration, achieved workload, raw evidence, distributions/time series, errors/resource signals, functional/recovery outcomes, comparison limitations, blockers/owners and next action. Use the report content below. Distinguish a completed plan or report from a passed performance requirement.

## Readiness and verdict

Every step needs a numbered outcome: `Complete`, `Not Applicable`, `Blocked` or `Pending`, with evidence or next action and exception reasons. Applicable workflow steps must be complete before claiming that assignment complete. A completed execution/review can legitimately return failure; procedural completion and passing performance are separate decisions.

For plan/build work, report plan/assets completed or blocked and execution unrun. For execution/review use:

- **`Performance-Passed`:** valid, sufficient evidence for the assigned conditions meets every applicable criterion, including achieved workload, functional correctness and required recovery. This is not release approval or an unbounded capacity guarantee.
- **`Performance-Failed`:** trustworthy evidence violates an applicable criterion. Preserve the failure even if other results are inconclusive; expose mixed case outcomes rather than hiding known failures behind an aggregate verdict.
- **`Performance-Blocked`:** required input, authorization, environment or evidence is unavailable and prevents the necessary evaluation.
- **`Performance-Inconclusive`:** a run exists but measurement validity, sample sufficiency, drift, noise or comparability prevents the claimed conclusion. An aborted run may prove a specific failure while leaving other questions inconclusive; state both.

An execution readiness gap cannot be marked Not Applicable to bypass a required check. Plans, scripts, missing runs, untrusted baselines and unqualified generators are not passing evidence. Never move acceptance thresholds after seeing the results without a separately accepted source change and re-evaluation.

## Selected guidance and ownership

Read [experiment and evidence guidance](references/experiment-and-evidence.md) only for the experiment, validity or report question in scope. Use `backend-resilience-standard` for approved overload/failover/recovery semantics, and `regression-testing-standard` when returning comparative evidence to unified regression. For client rendering/interaction budgets consume approved frontend performance decisions; do not treat HTTP load as proof of browser rendering or UX performance. Request missing instrumentation or environment decisions from their owners instead of changing live configuration. Return findings to the requesting user; this standard does not authorize extra agents or operations.

## Expected output

Use `performance-test-report.md` or the project's retained evidence format. Include the seven-step outcome record in that artifact, not a second register. Provide case-level verdicts and an appropriately scoped overall result, actual versus planned checks, aborts, cleanup, residual risk and evidence locations. For System Design contributions follow its diagram-first policy using a workload/measurement topology before explanatory notes; for measured reports use readable time-series/distribution charts when supported by actual data. Never invent charts, metrics or results.
